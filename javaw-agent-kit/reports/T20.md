# T20 Reader and StringReader

Implemented: `java.io.Reader` (abstract, `lock` field with both constructors, `read()`, `read(char[])`,
abstract `read(char[],int,int)`, chunked `skip(long)` with 8192-char buffer, `ready`, `markSupported`,
`mark`/`reset` "not supported" defaults, abstract `close`, `transferTo(Writer)`, `nullReader()` returning a
fresh anonymous reader per call, package-private static `checkFromIndexSize` with the JDK range message) and
`java.io.StringReader` (read, read(char[],int,int), long-arithmetic `skip(long)` that moves back but never
before the start, `ready`, `markSupported`, `mark` with the negative-limit check before the closed check,
`reset`, `close` without a `throws` clause). Registered both classes with `kit register`.

Left out: `java.lang.Readable` and `read(java.nio.CharBuffer)` on both classes (no `CharBuffer`/`Readable` in
the runtime), the package-private `Reader(Reader in)` constructor, Java 24 `Reader.of(CharSequence)`, and all
concrete readers other than `StringReader`. No `synchronized`/`volatile`: the JDK's `synchronized` methods are
replaced by plain methods on the single-threaded contract.

Notes: Passed `kit check` on the first run. The check-order pinning is the subtle part: `StringReader.read`
checks closed before the range, `nullReader().read` checks the range before closed, `StringReader.mark(-1)`
throws before the closed check, and `nullReader().transferTo(null)` throws before the closed check. `skip`
arithmetic stays in `long` until the bounded result is cast, which the `4294967298L` fixture exercises.
