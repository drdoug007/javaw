# T06 AbstractQueue and PriorityQueue

Implemented: `java.util.AbstractQueue<E>` (add, remove, element, clear, addAll on top of
offer/poll/peek) and `java.util.PriorityQueue<E>` (binary heap) registered in
`BytecodeExceptionRuntime`. PriorityQueue members: the four non-collection constructors, the
`Collection` constructor (copies `toArray()` into a new array, heapifies, keeps the comparator of a
`SortedSet`/`PriorityQueue` argument), `offer`, `poll`, `peek`, `remove(Object)`, `contains`, `size`,
`clear`, `comparator`, `toArray`, `iterator()`, plus private `siftUp`, `siftDown`, `removeAt`,
`grow`, `compare` and a snapshot-based `Itr`. `add`, `remove()`, `element`, `addAll`, `isEmpty`,
`toString`, `toArray(T[])`, `containsAll`, `removeAll`, `retainAll` are inherited from the runtime
`AbstractQueue`/`AbstractCollection`.

Left out: the `PriorityQueue(SortedSet)` and `PriorityQueue(PriorityQueue)` copy constructors (the test
verifies both are rejected before WAT generation; the `Collection` constructor handles both argument
types), `spliterator`, the `forEach`/`removeIf`/`removeAll`/`retainAll` overrides, and serialization
hooks (only the `Serializable` marker, no `serialVersionUID` or `writeObject`/`readObject`).

Notes: First attempt failed javac with a capture-conversion error — `((SortedSet<? extends E>) c).comparator()`
returns `Comparator<? super CAP>` and is not a declared subtype of `Comparator<? super E>`; the JDK avoids
this by casting the result to `Comparator<? super E>` explicitly, which I did in both `instanceof` branches.
After that the heap tests (heapOrder, equalPriorities, iteratorRemove, removeNeedsSiftUp) all matched the JVM
on the first Wasm run: the early `(Comparable<? super E>) e` cast in `offer` gives the empty-queue
ClassCastException, and the iterator walks a snapshot while `remove()` finds the last returned element in the
live array by identity.
