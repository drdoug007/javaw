# T35 InputStreamReader

Implemented: `java.io.InputStreamReader` (three constructors, `getEncoding`, `read()`, `read(char[], int, int)`,
`ready()`, `close()`), on top of the existing `Reader` and `InputStream`. The decoder lives entirely inside the
class: an 8192-byte buffer with per-fill compaction and one `in.read(bytes, byteLimit, 8192 - byteLimit)` call,
the `implRead`/`readBytes`/`decode`/`decodeLoop` loop with the at-most-once `in.available() > 0` block rule, and
UTF-8 / ISO-8859-1 / US-ASCII decoding with the JDK's per-sequence replacement counts. The two-char `read0()`
parks the low half of a decoded surrogate pair as the leftover, returned on the next `read()` even after
`close()`.

Left out: `InputStreamReader(InputStream, CharsetDecoder)` (no `CharsetDecoder` in the runtime) and
`read(CharBuffer)` (no `CharBuffer` in the runtime; checked by the test as unlinked). UTF-16, UTF-16BE and
UTF-16LE charsets throw `UnsupportedOperationException` where the JDK would decode them (see the docs). The JDK's
`sun.nio.cs.StreamDecoder` and its lock are not reproduced (single-threaded contract).

Notes: `kit check` printed PASS on the first run (~210 new lines, one file). The task's pseudo-code is the whole
algorithm; the stream-call log the test records (buffer length, offset, length and every `available()`) matched
the JDK on the first try once the compaction, the at-most-once `available()` rule and the single-`U+FFFD` tail for
an incomplete (not malformed) sequence at end of input were followed exactly.
