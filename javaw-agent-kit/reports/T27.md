# T27 PrintWriter

Implemented: `java/io/PrintWriter.java` registered in `BytecodeExceptionRuntime.java` — constructors
`PrintWriter(Writer)` and `PrintWriter(Writer, boolean)`; `protected Writer out`, `autoFlush`, `trouble` fields;
`write(int)`, `write(char[],int,int)`, `write(char[])`, `write(String,int,int)`, `write(String)`; all nine
`print`/`println` overloads plus no-arg `println` (line separator constant `"\n"`); `format`/`printf` (both
Locale forms) through `String.format`; covariant `append` forms; `flush`, `close`, `checkError` (with the
wrapped-`PrintWriter` branch), `setError`/`clearError`, private `ensureOpen`/`newLine`. Single-threaded: no
monitors, catches only `IOException`.

Left out: all constructors that do not take a `Writer` (`OutputStream`, `String`/`Charset`, `File` forms), the
`PrintStream` and `Formatter` fields, and the package-private `PrintWriter(Writer, Object)` constructor — their
dependencies (`OutputStreamWriter`, `File`, `PrintStream`, `java.util.Formatter`, `SharedSecrets`) are not in the
runtime. `System.lineSeparator()` replaced by the `"\n"` constant (no runtime `System.java`).

Notes: `kit check` passed on the first run; `kit guard` clean. Behaviour matched the spec exactly, including the
subtle cases: `write(String)`/`write(char[])` read the length before `ensureOpen` (NPE after close), `newLine`
writes to the underlying writer (subclasses never see the separator), `println(Object)` calls `print(String)`,
and `checkError` defers to a wrapped `PrintWriter`.
